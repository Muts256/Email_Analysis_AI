### A Note on the EU AI Act

This project is focused on whether AI makes the analyst's job faster and more effective; that remains the central question. However, it is important to acknowledge that deploying AI in a security operations context does not exist in a regulatory vacuum.
The EU AI Act, which came into force in August 2024, classifies AI systems used in critical infrastructure and cybersecurity contexts as high-risk.
This means that any organisation operating within the EU, or processing the data of EU citizens, that formally integrates AI into its SOC workflows would be subject to obligations around transparency, human oversight, accuracy, and auditability. In practical terms, this means
AI-assisted triage cannot simply replace human judgment; it must demonstrably support it, with the analyst remaining accountable for every decision the AI informs or makes.
This does not undermine the case for using AI in phishing triage. If anything, it reinforces it. The workflow documented in this project already treats AI as an analytical aid under human review and not an autonomous decision-maker. The analyst reviews every output, validates every IOC, and owns every verdict. That is not just good practice.
Under the EU AI Act, for organisations in scope, it is a requirement. For security teams considering formalising AI-assisted triage into their
workflows, awareness of the regulatory landscape is not optional. It is part of building something that is not only effective but defensible
